## Current direction

My current interests are in mathematical approaches to distill descriptions of complex systems, incidentally landing me at the intersection of theoretical condensed matter and biophysics. 

If one is to believe our world is directly reflective of the theories describing it (up to some approximation) then life at all levels must also condense to some mathematical system. Life is, for better or worse, noisy, and thus figuring out its workings is difficult. The forefront of my latest work has been in carefully pre-inspecting data before it is ever run through an analytical pipeline. Systematic discrepancies can lead to erroneous results; but even small, consistent discrepancies can uncover new phenomena. Another unusual feature of modern science is data abundance, and determining how to best exploit this abundance to reveal correlated artifacts in systems is also a research objective of mine.

My current work is in data analyses of bacterial auxotrophy, p-spin glass local approximations and machine learning applications to physics.


## Selected earlier work

### 2025–2026¹ | Beam-induced backgrounds

Lawrence Berkeley National Laboratory · US Muon Collider Collaboration. Studied oversampling methods that preserve particle correlations and machine learning approaches to generating background data.

### 2024–2025 | 2+1D Hamiltonian lattice QED

Princeton University. Derived and implemented a lattice model in Mathematica; computed ground-state energies, fermion bilinears, and mass gaps on a 2 × 2 lattice, reproducing perturbative results.

### SUMMER 2024 | Understanding a machine learning trigger

CERN · CMS. Investigated the CICADA Level-1 trigger through generated calorimeter deposit patterns and identified systematic biases associated with tower calibration.

### 2024 | Large-order quantum mechanics

Princeton University. Used instanton calculations to study correlation functions and large-order perturbative behavior in quantum-mechanical toy models.

### 2023–2024 | Axions & precision measurement

Princeton University. Reviewed axion detection experiments and investigated heading-error corrections in SERF magnetometers.
